ClimatePros · Stream 1 · Problem Definition

Three places a job leaks time — and what we automate

ClimatePros runs commercial refrigeration & HVAC field service: ~900–1,000 technicians, 46 branches, 10,000+ sites. Work orders arrive automatically. Everything after that still runs on a phone call and a technician's memory. Three use cases, three bottlenecks, one job.

Where AI sits: Use Case A is a deterministic algorithm — no LLM. It replaces a decision a dispatcher already makes by hand, so it must be explainable and repeatable. Use Cases B and C use AI, and in both the technician can always override it.

A

Dispatch — finding the right technician

Algorithm · no LLM Web portal · Dispatcher

A dispatcher has a job ready and ~40 technicians in the region. Picking one is manual, and confirming one means dialling them one at a time.

Step 1 · automated
Work order arrives
~95% by email → "Ready for Dispatch"
→
Step 2
Dispatcher opens board
Must filter first — unfiltered load 2–3 min
→
Step 3
Weighs distance, familiarity, availability, urgency
GPS map, 25-mi radius · prior-visit counts and current load sit behind separate clicks
→
Step 4 · bottleneck
Phones techs one-by-one
~2–3 min each · no answer → call next
→
Step 5
Assign
Drag-and-drop, fully manual
Why it hurts
The dispatcher juggles four factors at once — how far, how familiar, how soon free, how urgent — and every one of them is buried behind clicks or held in their head. Two dispatchers per branch are the throughput ceiling: more volume means more headcount.
We solve
Rank techs automatically on the same four factors the dispatcher uses today — distance, number of prior visits to the site (familiarity), time until they're free, and the job's priority (emergency / high / low). Push an accept/decline offer to the best one, cascade to the next on decline or timeout, escalate to a human only when the list runs out. Weights configurable per branch by a non-programmer. Pure rules and math — no LLM, so every assignment is explainable. Not route optimization — closest-available-responder, like a 911 dispatch.
C

Parts — finding the part that isn't on the truck

AI · tech overrides Mobile · Technician on-site

Customer said "the fridge isn't working." On site, the tech finds a leaking compressor and needs a part. Four places it could be. The first three the app already answers — the fourth is where the tech puts the phone to their ear.

Source 1 · works today
My truck
Every tech carries their own stock
→
Source 2 · works today
Nearby techs' trucks
Searchable on mobile — borrow from a tech near me
→
Source 3 · works today
Branch warehouse
One assigned warehouse per state
→
Source 4 · bottleneck
Third-party supplier — phones them, hopes they answer
Google search, 1–3 calls · billable hours burned · store product at risk
→
Failure
Writes a return dispatch
Second truck roll — job can't finish today
Why it hurts
The same physical part carries a different number at every vendor, and the supplier API matches on exact part number only — no fuzzy logic. So today a human bridges the gap by phone, under emergency pressure. This matching problem is the core technical risk.
We solve
Two steps, both on mobile. Suggest the part — AI proposes a shortlist from the problem and the equipment; the tech can add, swap or ignore it. Show where it is — one search across all four sources, each hit with its location and distance.

Where we stop: we show the part, the place and the distance. The tech decides where to pick it up — we don't rank by price and we don't order anything. Supplier API is already integrated; one supplier for the POC, more later.
B

Closeout — changing the job status

AI · tech overrides Mobile · Technician at end of job

Every job ends with a status change — completed, need parts, follow-up. It is mandatory, every single time. And behind that status change sits a wall of forms plus two free-text notes — one for the customer, one for the office.

Step 1 · every job
Tech changes job status
~13 statuses · often a machine room with no signal
→
Step 2 · bottleneck
Fills every form field and types both notes by hand
Doing it properly takes 10–15 min · voice dictation only ~30% accurate here
→
Step 3 · what actually ships
Tech skimps
~40% of 16.3M notes are ≤40 chars — "checked history" is the most common
→
Step 4 · downstream
Office can't bill or route
"Replaced motor" — but no motor in the parts list
Why it hurts
Note quality "ranges from excellent to fvdfdsfdsfds" and no quality rubric exists. The app already knows the metadata with zero typing — but what the tech found, the root cause, and the verified reading live only in their head.
We solve
Two halves. (1) Summarize — take the tech's rough words and restructure them into a clean, properly formatted note, split into what the customer sees and what the office needs. (2) Copilot — as the tech fills the form, suggest the likely values from similar jobs in history, the way an editor suggests the next line. The tech accepts or ignores each one.

The headline isn't prettier prose — that's table stakes. It's completeness: cross-check the note against the parts, time and photos actually logged, and surface what's missing. Never fabricates. Never blocks Save. Target: 10–15 min → ~15 s.

One job, three fixes

Work order in → (A) the right tech is offered the job on the web portal → tech arrives on site → (C) the missing part is found on mobile without a phone call → (B) the closeout note writes itself. Each removes a manual step that today costs either a dispatcher's hour, a technician's billable hour, or a second visit.

On the history B and C learn from. ClimatePros has 10–12 years of operational data — 200,000+ jobs in the last six months alone, and 16.3 million note records across the full span. It is messy by our own admission: ~40% of notes are ≤40 characters, and the most common note in the database is literally "checked history". That is the problem we're solving, so it is also the data we inherit. We will not train on the raw dump — the corpus gets de-duplicated down to unique cases first, and personally identifiable information is stripped before anything reaches a model.

Numbers sourced from discovery transcripts and read-only production/staging data. The 10–15 min → ~15 s figure (B) is the only before/after target currently claimed; A and C have baselines measured but no savings target agreed yet.
