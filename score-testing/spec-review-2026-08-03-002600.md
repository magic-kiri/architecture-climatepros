## Executive summary

**Overall score: 7.4/10 — Build with caveats**

This v1.3 is the "how" companion to the Stream 1 Scope Document, covering deterministic Auto Dispatch (A) plus AI-assisted Job Summarisation (B) and Parts Finder (C), and its own changelog shows it closing a prior round-2 tech-lead review (one Blocker, 25 Majors) with real engineering depth — fencing-token locking for A's assignment race, a fail-closed PII/pricing scrub with a CI-gating leakage eval, and an eval-gated CI/CD pipeline with per-runtime rollback. Cross-checking against the companion Scope Document confirms nearly all of that prior review's fidelity gaps are now closed (the cross-tier buffer, the Gemini contradiction, the CI/CD gap), but surfaces one this review is the first to flag: the completeness-gate language ("never blocks Save") drops scope B4's one hard exception — a missing, EPA-tracked refrigerant form must block submission. At 7.4/10 with no unresolved Blockers, the score is held down by seven Majors that are mostly external-dependency confirmations the document already tracks as its own Open Items (real Azure pricing, an existing telematics contract's coverage of new call volume, client DBA sign-off, on-prem hardware access) rather than places the architecture failed to think something through — a materially better position than hiding the unknowns, but it means the headline cost total and dispatch-capacity ceiling aren't independently verifiable yet. A team could start building from this today; a short, mostly ClimatePros/vendor-side punch list should close before full production reliance.

## Area detail

### 1. Overview & Problem Framing — 8/10
**Assessment.** The problem framing is crisp and consistent with the scope document's three bottlenecks, and the document adds a genuinely useful cover apparatus (document-control table, an explicit "how to read this" cloud-placement rule, and a 14-item Open Items appendix). B's success target is now correctly aligned with the scope document's own "up to roughly half" framing (resolving the prior review's numeric mismatch). The residual gap is that A and C's own success measures (scope §9) aren't restated or cross-referenced here, and the Table of Contents drifts out of sync with the document from §4 onward.
**Findings.** FND-010, FND-021.
**Suggestions.** Add a one-line cross-reference for A's and C's success measures in §1. Regenerate the Table of Contents' page numbers against the final v1.3 pagination.
**Strengths.** The document-control table, explicit reading rules, and Open Items appendix are unusually transparent framing devices; the B target now matches the scope document's own phrasing exactly.

### 2. Architecture & System Design — 8/10
**Assessment.** Three diagrams (unified, A-detail, B&C-detail) are clear, mutually consistent, and match the prose; every component has an explicit single responsibility, and the SQL-connection isolation is a clean, auditable boundary. The admission-control design for the AI gateway (semaphore sized to measured capacity, bounded queue, autoscaling on queue-wait not raw concurrency) correctly identifies the model server, not the stateless proxy, as the real bottleneck. Two smaller gaps: the tie-break's first criterion ("lowest ETA") doesn't quite match scope A2 Rule 5 ("fewer minutes left on the current job"), and the candidate-eligibility filter (workload limit, emergency-stacking) is asserted but not restated.
**Findings.** FND-008, FND-009.
**Suggestions.** Replace "lowest ETA first" with "fewest minutes left on the current job" in §6A's tie-break to match scope exactly. Add a paragraph confirming where the eligibility filter (A2 Rules 1 & 6) is evaluated.
**Strengths.** The fencing-token design for A's assignment race, the AWS/Azure/on-prem boundary rules, and the gateway admission-control design are all well above typical spec-stage rigor.

### 3. Feature Flows & System Sequencing — 7/10
**Assessment.** Close to the document's strongest section: full end-to-end flows with diagrams for all three use cases, and an exceptional unhappy-path treatment (offer-timeout cascade, XAUTOCLAIM worker-crash recovery, stale-fence-token rejection, a clean 409 contract for simultaneous acceptance, vendor-tier-drops-not-request for C). The one material gap: scope's B4/§7 states unambiguously (and twice) that the completeness gate has exactly one hard-block exception — a missing refrigerant form — while everything else stays a soft prompt; this architecture's flow description doesn't carry that exception forward, and §12's "never blocks Save" reads as an unqualified absolute.
**Findings.** FND-001.
**Suggestions.** Add the refrigerant-form hard-block rule to §5B/§6B, and carve it out of §12's "never blocks Save" statement, matching scope B4 Rule 4 verbatim.
**Strengths.** The per-use-case failure-mode tables (§11) and the fencing-token/XAUTOCLAIM recovery design are the most thorough unhappy-path treatment in the document.

### 4. Data Schema & Modeling — 6/10
**Assessment.** Structurally strong: Redis structures are fully specified; the MongoDB collections carry indicative field-level shapes with types, indexes, and a schemaVersion field for forward-only migration; the one SQL schema change is scoped precisely and sequenced expand→migrate→contract. Two real gaps: the vector-store seeding decision is open, with the document's own math showing the current sizing assumption stale by ~2 orders of magnitude; and no cascading-deletion mechanism is defined for B's non-rebuildable MongoDB records.
**Findings.** FND-002, FND-003, FND-011.
**Suggestions.** Resolve the §7C seeding decision and re-derive vector-store sizing/cost from the real 200,000+/6-month volume. Define a cascading-deletion mechanism for summaries/summary_edits, even an interim one.
**Strengths.** The Redis-structure table is a model of clarity for an ephemeral coordination layer; the schemaVersion + N/N-1 reader-compatibility rule for Mongo is a sound forward-migration approach.
