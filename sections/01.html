1. Stream 1 Overview

ClimatePros runs commercial refrigeration & HVAC field service: ~900–1,000 technicians, 46 branches, 10,000+ sites. Work orders arrive automatically. Everything after that still runs on a phone call and a technician's memory. Three use cases, three bottlenecks, one job.

Where AI sits: Use Case A is a deterministic algorithm — no LLM. It replaces a decision a dispatcher already makes by hand, so it must be explainable and repeatable. Use Cases B and C use AI, and in both the technician can always override it.

A Algorithm · no LLM

Auto-Dispatch — finding the right technician

Web portal · Dispatcher

A dispatcher has a job ready and ~40 candidate techs in the region. The four things that decide the right one — how far, how familiar with the site, how soon free, how urgent — are each buried behind separate clicks or held in the dispatcher's head. Confirming one means dialling candidates one at a time, ~2–3 min each; no answer → call the next.

Why it hurts: two dispatchers per branch are the throughput ceiling — more job volume means more headcount, not faster dispatch.

We solve: rank on those same four factors; push an accept/decline offer that cascades automatically. No LLM — every assignment explainable.

B AI · tech overrides

Job Summarisation Copilot — changing the job status

Mobile · Technician at end of job

Every job ends with a mandatory status change, and behind it sits a wall of forms plus two free-text notes — one for the customer, one for the office. Done properly it takes 10–15 min, often in a machine room with no signal where voice dictation is only ~30% accurate.

Why it hurts: so the tech skimps — ~40% of 16.3M notes are ≤40 chars, the most common note is literally "checked history". Downstream the office can't bill or route ("Replaced motor" — but no motor in the parts list).

We solve: restructure rough words into a clean customer/office note and suggest form values from similar jobs; flag what's missing. Never fabricates, never blocks Save. Target: 10–15 min → ~15 s.

C AI · tech overrides

Technician Parts Finder — finding the part that isn't on the truck

Mobile · Technician on-site

On site the tech needs a part. Three sources the app already answers — truck, nearby techs, warehouse. The fourth, a third-party supplier, is a phone call: Google search, 1–3 calls, hope they answer.

Why it hurts: the same physical part carries a different number at every vendor, and the supplier API matches exact part number only — no fuzzy logic. A human bridges that gap by phone under emergency pressure; miss it and it's a return dispatch — a second truck roll, job can't finish today.

We solve: AI suggests the likely part; one search shows where it is across all four sources with location and distance. No price ranking, no ordering.

Work order in → (A) the right tech is offered the job on the web portal → tech arrives on site → (C) the missing part is found on mobile without a phone call → (B) the closeout note writes itself. Full step-by-step diagrams for all three: see §5 Operational Flows.
